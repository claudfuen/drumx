# Coached pulse: the next M1 acceptance gate

**Outcome:** a player connects a kit, understands the first lesson and its pace, plays uninterrupted phrases, earns a meaningful next step, and returns with their progress intact. The same candidate must look intentional on Mac and Windows. This is the next acceptance gate, not a commitment to expand the curriculum first.

## Candidate review

Use the same commit on both systems. Use a separate test player in the native Mac app; the shared port has its own local save and does not import the native archive. Keep personal practice history out of screenshots and the repository.

| Check | Evidence to capture |
| --- | --- |
| First impression | Main menu, featured lesson, and preparation at the default size, 1020×780, and a larger monitor. Check title hierarchy, readable notation, pace card, primary action, and secondary routes. |
| Controls | Mouse hover, keyboard focus, pressed/disabled states, Escape during practice and from menus, and settings return. The primary action must remain visible at the minimum size. |
| Play | A whole 16-bar phrase, with one timing line, stable perspective, soft horizon entry, and restrained catcher feedback. Check a correct hit, extra hit, softer strike, hidden bar, and click-only feedback. |
| Result | Current BPM and assistance beside the score, an understandable checkpoint rule, one useful next action, a repeat action, and explicit free practice. |
| Return | Close and reopen. Verify saved scores, course access, guided plan, and the separate free-practice choice. |

Automated geometry, layout, source execution, and packaged execution checks support this review. They do not substitute for inspecting the actual rendered app. Windows display scaling and physical MIDI/audio need a real Windows session.

## First kit session

1. Connect the module and open **Settings**. Select the input; strike hi-hat, snare, and kick softly and firmly. Confirm the expected pad lights up. Fix aliases before starting. Choose app monitoring or the module's sound route and listen for accidental doubling.
2. Open **Find the pulse**. A new player should see a coached 60 BPM opening and a 16-bar phrase. An existing player may return to free practice; choose **Return to guided** / **Coached practice**. Explain what the primary action will do without opening a manual.
3. Play the whole phrase. The app must keep the tempo fixed, count you in, and avoid breaking after four bars. Judge comfort and readability before chasing a score.
4. Follow the suggested next pace, or repeat if needed. A strong opening phrase can suggest 66, then 72 BPM. **Try the checkpoint** lets an experienced player go directly to 72.
5. At 72, verify that one good take does not claim repeatable control. Two qualifying among three comparable takes should open the second lesson. Five stars at 60 must remain a valid score without opening an unattempted checkpoint.
6. Try hidden bars and click-only at the same pace. Notice whether the quiet highway still anchors time and whether the result distinguishes these conditions from guided playing.
7. Choose free practice, change the pace and phrase length, then return to guided. Each route should remember its own settings. Faster 84/96 BPM challenges are optional after the checkpoint.
8. Unplug or change the kit on review. The old score remains attached to its original setup; accepting a recommendation must reevaluate the new setup. Already-earned course access must remain available.
9. Close and reopen. Confirm the separate practice choices and comparable personal best. Report any point where the next action was unclear.

## What to report

Record candidate commit, OS/display scaling, module, USB/MIDI route, audio output, monitoring choice, and the exact screen or action. Useful feedback includes: opening pace too slow/fast; phrase too short/long; ambiguous pad mapping; visual clutter or clipping; missed/doubled sounds; unclear checkpoint; and uncertainty about what to do next.

A subjective report that play feels delayed is valuable. Call it an observation until pad-to-sound or pad-to-display timing has been measured with a stated method. MIDI evidence cannot verify grip, posture, rebound, or which hand played.

## Exit decision

Close this slice when a beginner can complete the loop without developer help, an experienced player can reach the checkpoint without unnecessary repetition, and both rendered apps pass the visual comparison. Then apply what we learn to the remaining foundation lessons. Keep M1 open until setup, the whole unit, return visits, and physical-kit reliability have been observed.
