# Curriculum review and expansion gates

**C1 active: a coherent 20-lesson introductory course.** Content expansion runs alongside M1 and the [premium quality gate](premium-quality-gate.md). More lessons do not close visual, hardware, reliability, or learning acceptance. The current course is mapped in [curriculum.md](curriculum.md); the next capability gate is C2 below.

## The strongest case for the original 12

The first unit makes a sensible promise: learn a usable backbeat, understand its counts, and try a variation without changing the instrument layout. It separates pulse, subdivision, rests, foot timing, and two-part coordination before combining them. When a groove breaks down, the learner already has a simpler repair exercise. Berklee's teaching guidance supports isolating relationships within a beat, recombining them, and establishing the groove before adding fills. [Berklee: learning a new beat](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/basic-funk-for-drums/)

The unit also builds useful product foundations: shared musical events for sound, notation, and assessment; explicit counts and rests; recorded assistance; separate self-checks; and a repeatable practice/review loop. The quarter-note groove, quiet ending, and short fill introduce meaningful changes rather than treating increased note density as the only form of progress.

Those are reasons to preserve the original musical content and saved history, not reasons to claim the learning outcome has already been observed.

## The strongest objections

| Objection | What this expansion addresses | What remains unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The first complete backbeat arrives after six preparatory steps | Existing repair sequence stays intact; new content adds a visible destination beyond it | Observe whether beginners need all six and experienced players can reach useful work without tedious repetition |
| There are no explicitly taught named rudiments | Name the existing singles exercise; introduce doubles and paradiddle, each with an orchestration | Physical sticking and controlled rebound remain self or teacher observations |
| More repetitions can masquerade as more content | Give each new lesson a distinct outcome, listening focus, repair route, and application | The suggested 174 minutes are repetition guidance, not unique lesson duration or measured learning time |
| A timing game can reward one strong limb while another disappears | New qualification checks each required instrument, plus on-time notes, extras, and repeated comparable takes | The thresholds still need evaluation with real learners and modules |
| The course teaches isolated loops | Add anticipations, offbeat relationships, and orchestration inside the supported one-bar model | No multi-bar structure, occasional fill placement, or musical accompaniment yet |
| Reading questions can be answered from prose | Ask concrete count/staff relationships and retain readable notation | These are concept checks, not unseen sight-reading tests |
| Guided accuracy can become visual dependence | Preserve reduced-guidance and click-only routes with separate evidence | No verified later-session recall or broader musical transfer yet |

The old access rule illustrates the coordination risk: the first backbeat has eight hats, two snares, and two kicks per bar. Missing every kick still matches 10 of 12 targets, or 83.3%, enough for an aggregate 80% rule. An expanded course should not call that three-instrument readiness.

## Why these eight lessons

Chapter four teaches **Double Stroke Open Roll** and **Single Paradiddle** as slow eighth-note studies, then moves each suggested sticking between hi-hat and snare. Chapter five adds a foot beneath the paradiddle, four-on-the-floor coordination, a kick before the barline, and offbeat hats. The original Single Stroke Roll is reused, not duplicated. This adds vocabulary and distinct coordination demands without pretending the current renderer supports sixteenths or multi-bar notation.

The [PAS reference](https://pas.org/rudiments/) supplies conventional rudiment names and stickings. The particular eighth-note exercises, order, counts, and teaching language are Drumx's choices. Yamaha's beginner exercises provide a precedent for moving a paradiddle from one surface to several and adding bass-drum coordination. Neither source validates Drumx's automated gates. [Yamaha practice exercises](https://hub.yamaha.com/drums/d-how-to/practice-exercises-for-the-beginning-drummer/)

### Counterarguments to this choice

- The two snare introductions have identical timing streams. Their difference is suggested sticking, which MIDI cannot verify. The follow-on orchestrations provide audible and assessable instrument changes, but still do not establish hand choice.
- Slow evenly spaced doubles do not teach every mechanism of a fluent fast roll. Treat this as an introduction and retain a technique self-check.
- A paradiddle orchestration is a coordination study, not automatically a useful accompaniment. Its musical role needs to be taught later in a phrase.
- Offbeat hats may be a larger jump than the lesson count suggests. The authored 48 BPM start and repair route are hypotheses to observe, not proof of accessibility.
- Three instruments keep this expansion compatible and visually stable. They also limit the product's promise: full-kit geography, articulations, and broader musical styles still need work.

## C1: Current vocabulary expansion gate

**Player promise:** after foundations, a learner has meaningful next work in named rudiments and coordination, can understand the task and its result, and can resume a coherent path without a larger, more confusing menu.

| Acceptance criterion | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Distinct learning outcomes | Every new lesson has a concrete task, count, listening focus, and named repair exercise; each snare rudiment has an application |
| Honest musical presentation | Notation, sticking suggestions, demonstrations, and targets agree on the authored events; supported quarters/eighths and rests render correctly; unverified technique is labelled |
| Credible access decisions | Non-pulse qualification uses two qualifying takes within a comparable three-take window: exactly 16 Guided bars at authored BPM, per-required-pad 80% matched and 70% on-time, extras at most 10% overall; the pulse policy stays unchanged |
| Preserved history | Original IDs, versions, order, and events stay intact; existing access remains; corrections are upserted, conditions do not mix, and later difficult practice does not erase a genuinely earned window |
| Focused exploration | A paged 20-lesson browser retains legible targets, clear prerequisites, keyboard navigation, selection, and resume at minimum supported size |
| Platform integrity | The same candidate passes content, policy, migration, notation, native integration, and both actual exported Mac/Windows executable checks |
| Learner value | Observe a beginner and an already-playing tester understanding the next task, pace, result, repair route, and distinction between course readiness and technique mastery |

The percentages and repetition count are product hypotheses. Guided qualification is not recall; stars are not a general skill level. Compare attempts under their recorded input, mapping, monitoring, calibration, length, and assistance conditions. See [curriculum.md](curriculum.md#pace-and-course-access) for the player-facing policy boundary.

**Gate status: open.** Source and packaged checks can establish implementation integrity; they cannot replace learner observation. Interactive visual inspection is deferred by the current display constraints. Real-kit input/audio testing, measured frame behavior, and the premium gate's full practice journey remain open as well. Record evidence against one candidate build before closing C1; content count alone is insufficient.

## C2: Next phrasing and literacy gate

**Player promise:** hold a groove through a short musical phrase, place a fill at its intended point, return to one, and read a changed rhythm using conventional counts.

Implement this boundary before adding exercises that require it:

1. **Two- and four-bar musical identity.** Version phrase length and events explicitly. Define repetition and partial-final-phrase behavior so audio, chart targets, bar numbers, counts, and history agree. A one-bar loop must not be relabelled a four-bar composition.
2. **Stable multi-bar notation and guidance.** Show the active phrase and its structural position without changing kit geometry or creating another timing destination. Verify transitions, silent beats, and a fill that occurs only in the intended bar.
3. **Real sixteenth-note literacy.** Teach `1 e & a`, render correct note/rest values and beams, and validate demonstration/scoring agreement on both platforms. Introduce the subdivision before combining faster pace, denser notes, and a new sticking.
4. **Meaningful transfer and reading evidence.** Use an original changed final bar or unseen short staff task. Distinguish a terminology answer, guided performance, immediate recall, and a later first attempt before preview.
5. **A musical acceptance task.** Observe a learner keep three bars of a basic groove, fill in the fourth, return to one, and explain the counts. Require coherent timing and recovery evidence without claiming that exact-match scoring measures musical taste.

C2 should deepen the existing skills before opening a broad song catalog. Triplets, swing, grace-note rudiments, dynamic assessment, full-kit articulations, and accompaniment remain separate capability decisions. Original or appropriately licensed musical material is required; a public repository does not authorize copying lessons or recordings.

Sources checked September 12, 2026. These are original product decisions informed by primary teaching references, not a validated pedagogy or PAS endorsement.
